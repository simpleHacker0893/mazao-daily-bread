## Platform

web

## Users
- **Primary and only user for now:** the owner of a small business in Gikomba market, Nairobi, who sells goods, services or both. Examples: a mama mboga selling sukuma wiki and tomatoes, a kiosk owner, a boda rider doing deliveries, a phone repair fundi.
- **When they use it:** standing at the stall through the day, often one-handed, in bright sun, with customers waiting, on a low-cost Android phone (3 GB RAM, Chrome), with paid data and patchy signal.
- **The job:** record each sale, expense and deni as it happens, then know at any moment what they actually made today and who owes them.
- **Later, not now:** staff or family helpers recording on the owner's behalf. Chama leaders and partners see the public demo only (D-051); they have no account role.

## Product Purpose
Mazao is an offline-first bookkeeping app (a PWA) that replaces the merchant's paper daftari. Success means the merchant knows their real profit for the day in three seconds and trusts the number enough to act on it: restock, chase a deni, or cut an expense. The R0 pilot runs with 5 merchants in Gikomba from Mon 2026-10-26.

## Positioning
**Know your real profit.** Most small traders only know their takings: the cash in the tin and the M-Pesa messages. Mazao tells them what they actually kept today after the cost of the goods they sold and their expenses (Faida Halisi), right at the stall. Recording works with no network, cash is kept apart from M-Pesa, and money given on deni is never counted as money in. Everything else (deni lists, stock, the day report) supports that one number.

## Operating Context
- The merchant's existing tool is a school exercise book (daftari), written with a biro, holding sales, debts and names. Mazao's first-run flow imports that notebook.
- Money moves as cash and M-Pesa; selling on credit (deni) to known customers is normal, and so is paying suppliers on credit.
- Goods sell in market units: kilo, fundo, tray, gunia. Item names stay as merchants say them (sukuma wiki, mayai).
- Records are made in the moment, between customers. The end-of-day ritual is the Today report, shared on WhatsApp.
- Market fees (ushuru) and transport are everyday expenses.

## Capabilities and Constraints
- **R0 features:**
  - record sales (goods and services), expenses, deni and payments;
  - items and stock;
  - Overview (today) and the Today report;
  - PIN lock on the phone; sign-in with an email code or Google (Clerk);
  - backup to a server in the EU and restore on a new phone;
  - English UI.
- **Planned later:**
  - Kiswahili switch, suppliers and pending supplies (Q-019), sales analysis and projections (D-054);
  - repair jobs, reports, then local AI entry. Every AI-produced record needs explicit confirmation before saving.
- **Offline first:** app pages read only from the phone's local database. Saving never waits for the network; backup happens when signal returns.
- **Trustworthy money:**
  - integer cents in the app;
  - confirmed records are never edited, only voided and replaced;
  - balances and stock are derived from the records, never typed in;
  - all money maths lives in one tested domain layer.
- **One merchant account per phone.**
- **Privacy:** the PIN never leaves the phone. Nothing logs record contents or phone numbers.
- **Public demo (D-051):** a fictional merchant's 28 days of mock data, with no sign-in, saving nothing.
- **Pricing:** free during the pilot. Nothing is promised beyond that.
- **Undecided:**
  - whether market words (deni, Faida Halisi) appear in the English UI (current: plain English);
  - what "pending supplies" means (Q-019);
  - the final projection method (D-054 is interim);
  - ODPC registration (Q-001).

## Product Principles
1. **The real profit number comes first.** Every screen either feeds Faida Halisi or helps the merchant act on it.
2. **Never wait for the network.** If it works only with signal, it doesn't work at the stall.
3. **The numbers must be right.** Correctness beats speed of delivery; a wrong total costs more trust than a missing feature.
4. **Faster than the notebook.** Recording a sale has to be quicker than writing it in the daftari, or merchants go back to paper.
5. **Show, don't claim.** Use real screens, real units and the merchant's own words, and never stand-in proof.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
- **Sunlight and one hand:** readable in direct sun on a cheap screen, and usable one-handed. Tap targets are at least 48 px.
- **Phone first:** 360 px wide first; works at 200% text size without horizontal scrolling.
- **WCAG 2.2 AA** contrast and semantics. Motion respects reduced-motion settings.
- **Low-end devices:** small bundles and lazy-loaded heavy screens.
